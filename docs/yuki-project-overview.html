The shortest version

What are we building?

A creative toolkit for rehearsal and performance—not one fixed trick. Yuki should be able to explore an effect, move with it, keep what feels good, and eventually arrange those discoveries into a show.

01 · EXPLORE

Movement becomes color

Angle, spinning, acceleration, and activity can shape what the club displays while it is moving.

02 · REHEARSE

One club can carry several ideas. A press moves to the next scene so Yuki can test patterns without returning to the computer.

03 · PERFORM

Music and juggling meet

A song can drive the large visual story while the juggler performs movements designed for each moment.

What we proved

From mystery hardware to a creative instrument.

This moved quickly because each step was tested on the physical clubs. Failures were useful: they showed us where the real boundaries are.

1

We made the three clubs a safe, shared baseline.

Factory recovery copies were preserved, a stable public firmware version was installed, and all three clubs were brought online one at a time.

2

All three now appear together in BenTo over Wi‑Fi.

They can be controlled as a group or addressed individually. A dedicated portable show network is the eventual theatre-friendly setup.

3

Rolling a club around its long axis changed its color.

This was the first “oh wow” moment: the physical orientation of the prop became part of the effect, and matching colors appeared at repeatable points in the air.

4

A button-driven program ran inside a club.

The first test cycled red, green, and blue. It grew into four sensor scenes: orientation wheel, orbit comet, activity flame, and spin heat.

5

We learned to score complete songs.

BenTo can play music and light cues from the same clock. We now analyze a song’s sections, repeated ideas, builds, impacts, and rests before composing the lights.

6

We made several full performance studies.

They range from sparse Japanese koto to bass-heavy electronic tracks, Yuki’s existing performance song, and the newly prepared Heartaches show.

Dig deeper: what is BenTo?

BenTo is the Mac application that discovers the clubs, creates patterns and timelines, plays music, and sends live color information over Wi‑Fi. We can now generate a show as a file, load it into the running app, assign it to the clubs, and verify that it is ready—without manually rebuilding every cue.

The artistic lessons

The clubs taught us how they want to be composed.

These rules did not come from a screen. They came from watching the real props in a dark room and in motion.

☀

Never casually disappear

A juggling prop needs some light at nearly all times so the performer can catch it and the audience can follow its path. Fine detail rides over a visible base.

≡

Unison is the default

Because the clubs constantly trade places, one unexplained odd club looks like a mistake. They stay identical unless the difference is clearly part of the effect.

↑

Compose for the body

The large upper body shines more than the narrow handle. Important cues should fill the body or the whole prop, not hide in a tiny handle detail.

♥

Let the music have memory

When a musical idea returns, its visual family should return too—then grow brighter, faster, fuller, or stranger as the song develops.

◎

Movement can justify difference

If two clubs look different because Yuki is moving them differently, the audience can understand the cause. Sensor-driven variation feels intentional.

✦

Brightness is part of the drama

Low light protects visibility and battery. The peaks should still exploit how gorgeous, saturated, and bright these props can be.

Creative studies

Different songs teach different lessons.

These are not finished stage works. They are experiments we can watch, discuss, and improve with Yuki’s movement knowledge.

Acoustic / phrase-led

Kōjō no Tsuki

Moonlit koto phrases, drifting petals, lantern shimmer, and long musical breath instead of a rigid beat grid.

Electronic / music-linked

Gettosinfonía

Rising tones drive color, siren-like passages become bold red/blue vocabularies, and every club stays in unison.

Yuki’s performance track

Papa Was Stoned

A complete long-form visual interpretation that taught us why small unexplained differences between clubs are distracting.

New / trap experiment

Heartaches

Red double-heartbeats for the heartbreak passages, bright body-first color, and an intentionally unhinged heavy-trap overload.

First proof

Exit the Premises

Our first confirmation that one BenTo timeline could play a song and send authored color to a physical club.

Movement / local

Sensor Playground

Four button-selected ideas that turn orientation, orbit, activity, and spin into light directly on the club.

The important conversation

What does Yuki want the light to say?

The technology is ready enough to stop guessing in isolation. The next progress comes from pairing Yuki’s movement instincts with fast visual experiments.

Dig deeper: what remains uncertain?
  • Which sensor scenes still look legible at full juggling speed?
  • How precisely can three clubs stay aligned through an entire performance?
  • Should local movement response run alone, or inside music-selected scenes?
  • How should scenes resume after a club, computer, or show-network interruption?
  • Can persistence of vision create readable shapes or images during controlled swings?
